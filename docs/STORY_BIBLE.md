## 1. Logline, themes, tone

**Logline.** An ordinary man wakes up owing the House, a casino built over a wishing well, for a jackpot he doesn't remember winning. Under his name on the card is a second name: the girl across the hall he has never dared to speak to. In his head is a voice that knows every table in the House. She wants him out at any cost. The voice wants him to stay.

**Themes.**
- **Whose are you?** Your luck, your memories, your love: the game keeps asking which of them are yours.
- **A kept word.** The House runs on distrust. The one thing it cannot price is a kept word, and two people keeping the same word is the one thing it cannot collect.
- **Love that won't let go** is still love, and still dangerous.
- **What would you put on the table for someone else?** Everyone answers it. Ada staked her name for her sister, and stakes it again for you. Selim puts his on your quota. Mira stayed. The Collector signed. You stake yourself.

**Tone.** Dark, with dry humour: the House is polite, the Collector says thank you, the Compere is a ham, a receipt lists "one hand (left)". Home starts sincere and quiet, and then it gets too close. The House can be funny. The kitchen never is.

## 2. The world

**The Well (Kuyu).** An old wishing well under the city. Throw a coin, make a wish. It almost never answers. When it does, that is a jackpot. It grants on credit.

**The House (Kasa).** A casino built on the Well to collect what the Well is owed. It is a casino because that is the only place where people feed a hole in the ground every night and say thank you. Seven floors down (B7 to B1), then the Ground. Each paid Night the lift takes you one floor up. Night 8 is the street door. The lift is the one in your building: it has a B button nobody presses.

**The JackID card.** The receipt. Front: JACKPOT and a date. Back: your name and the debt, and under it, in small print, the second name. The card is always a little warm, and warmer when the second name is near.

**Who owns the card.** The House keeps the card and the Collector works the debt. The Three Seats own the House, and the Three Seats owe the Well. Everyone here is somebody's debtor. The bottom of the chain is a hole in the ground.

**The Law of the House** (five rules on the Clerk's plate, then in the journal):
1. A wager said over a card is binding. The Clerk hears it.
2. Anyone may cheat. A cheat proven to the Clerk loses the wager. A wrong accusation pays a fine.
3. Every card has two names: the one who wished, and what they wished for. When the first can't pay, the House takes back the second.
4. A loser at the House's own table pays in memory. The House cuts it out and files it on the next card printed, whoever's that is.
5. The lift takes up only the first name. A card burns only when the House loses a clean wager at its own table.

The plate leaves one thing out, and the Clerk says it only when asked: a wager nobody loses cannot be collected (the kept pair, §5a).

**Ranks.**
- **Guest (Misafir):** holds a card and pays the quota.
- **Chair (Sandalye):** sold part of their name for a table. Chairs play guests for the House and win their name back a piece at a time. None has finished on their own.
- **The champion (Şampiyon):** the Chair the House put up against any guest worth beating. For as long as anyone remembers, he never lost. Then Ada beat him (§9).
- **Still one (Donuk):** a name fully taken. They staff the floor, fill the seats and never blink.
- **The Clerk (Katip):** rules and records. The only thing in the House that owes nothing.
- **The Three Seats (Üç Koltuk):** the Lords of Ash (time), Ledgers (money) and Mirrors (faces), behind the Hollow Door. Owners, and bored.

**Debts paid in other things:**
- name seals (three per card; at zero the card reads BLANK and you go still)
- keepsakes (a hair tie, a watch, a photo strip)
- faces (always taken first)
- memories: the night of the wish (the House keeps the wish, you keep the debt), and a loser's whole table (Rule 4)
- words (a broken promise is written down as Heat, the Collector's 0-6 meter)

**Why everyone is a silhouette.** The House takes your face first, because a face is how people find you. Anyone written in the Clerk's book shows to a card holder as a black shape. The rim of light is what is left of their own name, in their own colour. The eyes glow because the House never takes eyes: a guest has to see the table. The rims tell you who someone is:

| Rim | Who |
|---|---|
| cold cyan | still ones |
| red | the Collector |
| warm amber | Ada (the only warm light on any figure) |
| phosphor green | Loki, and only in glass |
| white eyes | someone else is playing that person's hand |

Manga portraits show faces because reading a person gives their face back for a moment. That is why faces appear only in duels and close talks. The player sees Ada as a silhouette from the first scene and blames the light ("The light is wrong", intro.1). She is in the Clerk's book twice: as your second name, and as the only guest who ever beat the champion. It is the biggest clue in the game, in plain sight.

## 5. Mind games

### (a) ROCK, PAPER, PROMISE / TAŞ, KAĞIT, SÖZ

**Hook.** The sisters' game. Mira made it up so little Ada would stop cheating. The House took it over because it prices the one thing the House lacks: a kept word. Ada beat the House's champion with it.

**Rules (20 seconds).**
- Rock, paper, scissors. First to 3.
- Before each throw, the caller says what they'll throw: a promise. Callers take turns.
- Keep your word and win: 2 points. Any other win: 1 point.
- Break your word: your card cracks. Three cracks and you lose.
- **Me too:** the answerer may promise the same hand. If both keep it, that's a kept pair: a tie, no points. Three kept pairs end the game with no loser.

"Me too" is Ada's house rule. It isn't on the Clerk's plate, but the Clerk hears every word said over a card, so it holds at any table. It never helps you win, and liars never keep their half, so in practice it only happens between two honest players.

**Depth.** A promise has three answers:
- believe it, and counter;
- doubt it, and throw the same hand (it ties a kept promise and beats the smart break);
- go one step further, and beat the doubter.

Kept wins pay double, so honesty tempts; cracks stop endless breaking. The game is one read: does this person mean it, tonight, at this score? No limited hands: the only resource is your word.

### (b) THE QUESTION / SORU (bluff and read)

**Rules (20 seconds).** One card each, 1 to 9, face down. High card wins. Each of you asks one yes-or-no question about the other's card, and the answer may be a lie. Then one bet: raise, see or fold. If the cards are shown and you lied, you pay a fine. First to 5.

**Depth.** What you ask ("above 5?" or "odd?"), how you read the answer, how you answer. A lie pays only if they fold: a bluff with a price.

**Your own tell.** When you lie, your gloved hand trembles on screen, and the rival sees it too. Heat makes it worse, ŞANS steadies it, and "steady hands" food (W77) removes it for a Watch.

**Beginner: "Ask the Compere"** (floor event, N3+). He lies half the time on low cards, and every lie ends with "and that, friends, is the honest truth." Stake: half S. He is bad at his own game, and that is a clue (§9).

**Hard: the Collector (N6).** Two cards each, keep one, two questions each. He rarely lies. From round 2 or 3 his deck is marked, so he asks questions he already knows the answer to, to see whether you lie. The read is why he asked.
- Clues: TELL, his thumb along the edge. TABLE, nicks on high cards. MATH, 41 cards in a 40-card deck.
- Call: C, then MARKED DECK.
- Economy: seeing a raise made on a lie and winning +1 ŞANS (max 2). Win: Heat −2, his story, the Manager's Key. Lose: Heat +2.

### (c) THE CROOKED TABLE / HİLELİ MASA (find the cheat)

**Rules (20 seconds).** Tonight one of three tables is crooked. Play them and watch. When you're sure, call CHEAT at that table. Right: the House pays back double what you lost there. Wrong: the Clerk fines you and Heat goes up.

**Format.** A floor event in the wave 75 family. At Watch 1 the Compere names three tables, each with a readable brass UNDER WATCH plate; Tamsin runs the rig. A clue rings a soft bell and your hand jots it into the Tally Sheet (the journal page). To call, face the table and press C; the Clerk cuts in and the reveal plays. Clues use the duel channels:

| Channel | Examples |
|---|---|
| TELL | a still-one dealer blinks; Tamsin's eyes open; the Compere stutters |
| TABLE | a fourth cherry on a three-cherry reel; a ball that skips a pocket; a warm card |
| MATH | Tally Sheet: "0 cherries in 15 pulls, about 3 expected" |

**Beginner (N2).** One loud rig: the fourth cherry, the stutter, and a Tally line by the fifth pull.

**Hard (N6+).**
- **A decoy:** a fair table runs cold. The Sheet says "unlucky, not crooked: 1 in 6".
- **A quiet rig,** such as a fifth king in the shoe that shows only in the maths.
- **Ride the rig:** once you know its pattern (the crooked plinko pays every fifth ball to keep guests hooked), play it instead of calling. Every three rig plays: Heat +1. Calling is safe and riding is greedy: pillar 3's House face.

**Economy.** Right call: refund ×2 (capped at 25% of the quota), +1 ŞANS, Heat −1 (the Collector likes a guest the House owes). Wrong call: a fine of S and Heat +1.

## 9. Loki and the champion

Client brief: a Joker who helps us, who doesn't always tell the truth, and whom we have to keep sweet; use him too much and we go mad. Rev. 2: he is the stolen memory of a gambler who lost once, and the rest of that gambler is a table we have to beat. The numbers are in docs/JOKER.md. Original IP: only the spirit of a charming, malevolent thing that shares the hero's head.

**The champion.** For longer than anyone on the floor remembers, the House put one Chair up against every guest worth beating, and he never lost. He read people the way other men read signs. He taught Lucan the watch, marked Orrin's deck, built half of Tamsin's rigs and dealt through her hands. On Night 0 Ada sat down with him at Rock, Paper, Promise. She called paper at match point and kept it. He broke to rock, as he always had at match point, and lost. Rule 4 did the rest.

**The split.** A court card is one figure printed twice, upside down. So is he.

| | The voice (Loki), in you | The rest of him (the Compere) |
|---|---|---|
| Has | every table: reads, tells, rigs, his hands, his jokes | his name, his body, the floor's memory of him, and the night he lost |
| Lacks | his name and his loss: he believes he has never lost | every read: he can't win anymore, so he talks |
| Wants | a name to ride up on, and yours is the nearest (Rule 5) | himself back, which means beating you for it at the House's table |
| Fears | the rest of him, and Ada, the one hand he can't read | the floor looking away |

**What is yours and what is his.** Your amnesia is one night: Night 0, which the House kept as the price of the wish (Mimosa holds it). Everything before it is yours, only quiet: a rented flat, a dull job, months of a door across the hall. His memory sits on top of yours and is louder, ten thousand nights of tables, so you remember the felt better than your own street.
- **His:** the ease at every machine, every read the voice whispers, the hand in a takeover, the grin, the urge to raise.
- **Yours:** the kitchen, the dishes, the small promises, Ada, and the coin.

**Is your love for Ada yours?** Yes, and the game shows it three ways, one per act.
1. He can't talk in the kitchen. He has no memory of any kitchen, and none of her except the dread of a hand he can't read.
2. N7's flashback: you followed her down and threw the coin for her before he was in you. He arrived in the ripple after.
3. In the true ending, with the borrowed luck gone, she beats you at her game: "There you are." The man she loves is the one who loses at her table and keeps his word.

The voice says otherwise ("We picked her"), and that line carries his tell. The answer can still be lost: every takeover lets his memory write further in (Ink), and from Ink 8 he starts to remember her kitchen as his. Love here isn't a memory. It's a kept word, the one thing he never had.

**Loki, the voice.**
- **The name.** "Loki" is borrowed: an old trickster's name he wears like a stolen coat. He won't give his real one, because he doesn't have it: it stayed with the rest of him. He'd rather joke ("Names are expensive here, ortak.") than admit that.
- **Where he lives.** On the face of the card, the cold side. The back, with your names, is warm.
- **Look.** No body. He shows only in glass: the lift mirror, machine screens, the peephole lens, the photo frame, the home PC. Under your reflection, upside down like the bottom half of a court card, sits a second head with a flat face (the face from the champion's poster), a phosphor-green rim and a grin.
- **When he takes your hand.** The glove comes off, and the fingers under it are long and ink-black: the champion's hands, as he remembers them. In a reflection, your eyes go white.
- **Voice.** Fast, friendly, petty. He gossips about everyone, calls you "ortak" (partner) and says "sen". On the floor he is funny. In the kitchen he is silent.
- **Lies when it suits him.** The big one comes on Night 1: "When the card burns, we walk out too." When the card burns, he burns with it.
- **The tell.** He can't lie alone: every lie has "we" in it. His truths are memories, his own, so they say I and you. His lies are plans, and every plan he has is the two of you as one.
- **Madness.** Every takeover darkens a fingertip: his memory writing over yours. Five black tips and the hand is his.

**How the cast sees him:**
- **Ada** beat him. She can't see him, but she knows his smile when it's on your face, and from N6 she talks to him directly. If you admit him to her (N5, once), she makes soup every evening.
- **Mira** watched her sister beat him. She teaches you the tell.
- **Selim** poured his drinks for years.
- **The Collector** collected his winnings, then carried him to your card. He calls him Mr Loki, and finds the name funny.
- **Tamsin:** he dealt through her, and her eyes went white.
- **Lucan:** his understudy. The watch was his.
- **The Clerk** holds the record of Night 0 and can't be lied to. Loki hates that.
- **The Compere:** Loki can't stand him and can't say why.
- **Mimosa** keeps his poster and two of his marked cards: "Somebody lost a whole deck down here. Only the faces."
- **Vell** counted him into the Well.

**At the kitchen table (N8).** Below Ink 8 he has nothing to say. Above it, he wants her gone: "Win. She asked for it." It's true, and that is what makes it dangerous.

**At the Burning Wager.** He whispers before each of the Champion's throws, about his own old habits; nobody knows them better. Neither result suits him: a clean win burns him with the card, and the Champion's win swallows him back into the man who lost. He wants a dirty win, so the card stays and he rides up with you. So his lies push you toward a crack, or toward his hand. At Trust +3 he gave you his word over the card, and Rule 1 binds it. Spend it at match point and he has to tell the truth against himself: "He'll break. I always did." If he takes your hand, the throw wins, the Clerk says "That is not your hand", the Champion says "That hand is mine", and the card stays. He can win you any game. He can't win you a clean one.

## 10. Bonds (Bağlar)

Client note: friendships matter. One plain value per key character, not a pile of meters.

**The value.** A bond of 0..3 for Selim, Mira, Tamsin, Lucan, Mimosa, Vell and the Collector. Selim starts at 1, the rest at 0. Ada's bond is Harmony (0..5) and Loki's is Trust (−3..+3); both already exist. The Clerk and the Compere have none: the Clerk can't be befriended, and the Compere is the rest of Loki.

**Shown plainly.** The Tally Sheet's PEOPLE page shows each silhouette in its rim colour with three pips. A move shows a toast in their voice ("Selim will remember that."), never a number. Bonds never move ŞANS: stars stay home's (pillar 2).

**How it moves** (one +1 per character per Night):
- **Help them:** their quest or errand (Mimosa's ledger, Vell's tapes, Selim's short till on N4).
- **Keep your word to them:** a promise made on the phone or over a table, checked at Dawn like any promise.
- **Share winnings:** a tip at the bar, a stake covered, a hotline bargain paid for someone else.
- **Show up:** their floor event, their side duel, a visit to the Wash.
- **Defend them:** stand up for Selim to Ada (N6), cover him when the Collector leans on him (N4), refuse to call a cheat on Tamsin that you can't prove.

- **Neglect:** skip someone three Nights running and they lose a pip.
- **Betrayal** (a broken word to them, a false accusation, letting Selim pay and never paying him back): straight to 0, and they act against you once on N8.
