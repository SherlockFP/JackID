# JackID: Sales Prep Kit (2026-10-19)

Everything needed to put JackID on Steam: copy, tags, price, screenshots, capsule and trailer briefs, launch checklist, honest gaps.
Docs only. Facts about the game come from `docs/STORY_BIBLE.md`, `BLESSINGS.md`, `JOKER.md`, `ART.md`, `GDD.md`, `HANDOFF.md` and the code.
Steam rules and prices were looked up on 2026-10-19; links are in the Sources section at the end. Anything marked **(est.)** is my estimate, not a checked fact.

**Facts the copy leans on.** First-person PSX casino roguelike (three.js, Vite web app). 8 Nights, a quota each Night, 12 core machines opening Night by Night
(`progression.ts`) plus a Free Casino sandbox with the rest. Duels: Rock, Paper, Promise; The Question; Crooked Table. Loki (the voice that lies with "we"),
Ada (the girl across the hall), Selim (the bartender), 7 bonds. 3 name seals as lives, boons from the House's owners (Well, Ash, Ledgers, Mirrors).
9 endings, one hidden. EN and TR. 30 achievements. Procedural, adaptive music (no music licences). Calm Mode and a photosensitivity switch.
Co-op/Versus (2-8, P2P) exists but is not verified on two real PCs, so the copy below does not sell it. **No gamepad support exists today.**

### Long description, EN

You wake up with a card in your pocket. JACKPOT on the front, a date. Your name on the back, and a debt under it. Under your name there is a second name in small print. You don't remember winning anything. The House does.

JackID is a first-person casino roguelike that looks like 1997 and plays like a deadline. The House is a hotel casino that has been open all night for fifty years, built over a wishing well. It has one rule: whatever the Well gave you on credit, you pay back.

**Pay the quota, one night at a time.** Eight nights, each number bigger than the last. Twelve machines open up as the nights go on: coin flip, hi-lo, video poker, mines, plinko, keno, blackjack, roulette, a night derby, and a big red slot cabinet called the Big One. Bank your coins, watch the interest, and keep an eye on the clock when Last Call comes.

**Play people, not only machines.** Rock, Paper, Promise is a sisters' game: say what you'll throw, then keep your word or break it. Catch a cheat at the Crooked Table. Read a bluff in The Question. Every rival has a tell, and every tell is written down somewhere if you look.

**Keep the voice honest.** Loki lives in your head and has stood at every table in the building. He is fast, friendly and useful. He also lies, and every lie has a "we" in it. Lean on him and your gloves start to go black.

**Walk home.** Each dawn the lift takes you one floor up. You cross the wet street, and two soft knocks come at the door: Ada from across the hall, who will get you out of this at any cost. Selim pours at the lobby bar and tells you to eat something. Not everyone who loves you wants the same thing.

**Borrow from the owners.** At dawn the House's gods offer three favours and you keep one. A favour is a debt. Your name stands on three seals, and a missed quota costs you one.

**Nine endings.** Some of them you earn by losing. One is hidden, and nobody will tell you how.

**Built plain.** Full English and Turkish text. Low-poly, 15-bit colour, wobbling vertices. Calm Mode turns the scares off. The music is made live in the game and shifts with the stakes. There is no real money anywhere: no cash-out, no purchasable coins, no loot boxes.

**Turkey and TRY.** Steam moved Turkey to USD pricing on 2023-11-20 (announced 2023-10-25). TRY is not on Steam's live currency list; Turkey sits in the discounted **USD_MENA** group. So there is no TRY price to set. Set the USD_MENA price instead: **USD 4.99 (est.)**, about half the base. The only evidence is Buckshot Roulette's Turkish listing at USD 1.49 against 2.99, and that may have been a sale. At about 48.4 TRY per USD (observed 2026-09-04; forecasts for October run 48 to 50), USD 4.99 is about **240 TRY** and USD 9.99 about **484 TRY (est., recheck the rate on the day)**. If we also sell outside Steam in TRY (own site, itch), 249 TRY mirrors the USD_MENA tier.

**Fee maths.** USD 100 recoups at USD 1,000 adjusted gross revenue, about 100 copies at 9.99. Valve's cut is 30% up to USD 10M, then 25% and 20% (2018 tiers; recheck on Steamworks).

## 5. Capsule art brief

### Sizes and Steam rules (checked on the Steamworks asset pages)
Header 920x430. Small 462x174. Main 1232x706. Vertical 748x896. Library capsule 600x900 (this is the 600x900 vertical you asked for). Library hero 3840x1240, **no text**, keep the centre 860x380 safe. Library logo 1280 wide, transparent PNG.
Capsules may hold artwork, the game name and an official subtitle only. No review quotes, awards, sale text, "wishlist now", "coming soon", other products. PG-13 art. Make the title big enough to read at the small size.

### The idea
The JackID is a laminated card with two names. The key image is **a court card**: you on top, Loki upside-down underneath, the way the bottom half of a court card is the top half turned over. It carries the whole pitch in one shape, a man and the thing in his head.

### Composition
- **Header 920x430.** Landscape. Left third: the JACK·ID marquee logo. Right two thirds: a gloved first-person hand (fingertips going black) holds the card up, tilted, in front of a red Big One cabinet blurred into oxblood damask and gold ceiling coffers. On the card's glossy face, a phosphor-green upside-down grin. Far back in a warm doorway, one silhouette with a thin amber rim (Ada). Light from the top left, warm; card edge lit.
- **Small 462x174.** The logo only, filling the frame on the dark oxblood plate with the bulb dots, a corner of the card in the lower right, one green dot (the grin). It must read at 231x87. No scene.
- **Vertical 748x896 and library 600x900.** The court-card layout. Logo at the top fifth. Below, the lift: brass doors half open, one bare cream bulb, a smoked mirror. In the mirror, the player's gloved reflection above and the upside-down green grin below, split by a thin brass line like a card's centre. A single amber-rimmed silhouette stands behind the player's shoulder.
- **Hero 3840x1240 (library).** The street at night from the casino door, wet, KASINO in pink neon, a taxi, nothing in the centre safe area but the door's glow. No text.

### Palette (from `src/kit/palette.ts`, `ART.md`)
60% dark: oxblood `#5a1019`, dried blood `#3a0810`, walnut `#311910`, pit black `#101019`. 30% metal and warm light: gold `#c59c42`, gold shine `#efd67b`, brass `#8c6b29`, bulb yellow `#ffce5a`. 10% accents, one per image: neon magenta `#ff42a5` (marquee and sign), Loki green `#8dff9a` (only for the grin and the rim), amber `#e69431` (Ada's rim only). Cyan `#63e6de` is for the still ones; leave it out of key art. Never clean, never flat.

### Look
PSX honest: render at a quarter of the final size (for example 230x108 for the header) with nearest-neighbour upscale, a 15-bit palette, ordered dither, wobbling vertices, no smooth bloom. Glow is additive and capped. A little CRT grain is fine. No real playing-card faces, no real banknotes, no real-money symbols.

### Logo treatment
Use the menu marquee as built: cream-gold pixel letters, **JACK:ID** with the stacked-dot separator, on a dark oxblood plate with a row of bulb dots either side (see `tour92\01-menu.png`). Ship three files: the plate version for capsules, a flat transparent version for the library logo (1280 wide), and a one-colour version for the small capsule. Keep a hard 1 px dark outline so it survives a bright background. No tagline in any capsule; the hook belongs in the description.

### Desktop build: Electron vs Tauri vs NW.js
The game is a Vite web app (`dist` is 80 MB, `public/assets` 75 MB, mostly assets). It loads over HTTP today (`/assets/...`, `/version.json`, the lobby proxy), so any wrapper needs a custom protocol or a relative base, and the version poll turned off.

| | Electron | Tauri | NW.js |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size added to the 80 MB **(est.)** | about 150 to 250 MB unpacked (a bundled Chromium); about 70 to 90 MB compressed in the depot | about 5 to 10 MB shell (Tauri says under 600 KB for a bare app) | about 130 to 250 MB, like Electron |
| WebGL consistency | Same Chromium for every player, so the PSX shaders look the same everywhere | System webview: WebView2 on Windows, WebKitGTK on Linux. Three engines to test; unknown on Deck | Bundled Chromium, same as Electron |
| Steamworks | `steamworks.js` (MIT): achievements, cloud, overlay helper `electronEnableSteamOverlay()`, documented Electron setup | No built-in support; needs a Rust plugin over `steamworks-rs` or FFI | Named in steamworks.js topics, no setup shown; greenworks is legacy |
| Steam overlay | Works with the Electron helper | Known problem: overlay does not draw over WebView2 child windows (WebView2 feedback thread; Construct dropped its workaround) | Reported to need testing; people moved NW.js to Electron to fix it |
| Effort **(est.)** | 1 to 2 days to wrap and run; 1 to 2 more for Steam bridge, cloud file, achievements | 4 to 7 days with real risk on the overlay | 2 to 3 days, uncertain overlay |
| Risk | Size; renderer needs care: keep `contextIsolation` on and call Steam from the main process, not the page | Overlay and Linux webview | Smaller community; steamworks.js docs lean Electron |

**Recommendation: Electron with steamworks.js.** It is the only option where the overlay, achievements and cloud have a documented path, and WebGL is the same everywhere. Size is a one-time cost on a USD 9.99 game. Call Steam from the main process and bridge it by IPC. Ship the folder through SteamPipe, no installer. Pause the game on blur yourself (Electron throttles background tabs by default).
